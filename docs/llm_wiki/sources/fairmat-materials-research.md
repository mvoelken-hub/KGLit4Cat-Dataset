# FAIRmat Materials Research

**Authors:** Matthias Scheffler, Martin Aeschlimann, Martin Albrecht, Tristan Bereau, Hans-Joachim Bungartz, Claudia Felser, Mark Greiner, Axel Gross, Christoph T. Koch, Kurt Kremer, Wolfgang E. Nagel, Markus Scheidgen, Christof Woll, Claudia Draxl  
**Year:** 2022  
**Venue:** Nature 604:635-642  
**File:** `docs/literature/s41586-022-04501-x.pdf`

## Abstract
Perspective on FAIRmat, an NFDI consortium building FAIR data infrastructure for condensed-matter physics and chemical physics of solids. The paper argues that materials data become useful for AI and discovery only when comprehensively described with metadata, ontologies, workflows, provenance, and repository infrastructure.

## Key Findings
- Materials science faces big-data challenges of volume, variety, velocity, and veracity; most data are not sufficiently findable or interoperable without metadata and ontologies.
- FAIRmat uses NOMAD as a blueprint and extends it toward synthesis, experiments, theory, computation, and federated materials infrastructure.
- NOMAD converts computational materials data into a common form, supports upload/download, embargoes, a materials encyclopedia, and AI tooling.
- FAIRmat identifies four implementation challenges: community adoption, metadata/ontology development, data processing and analysis tools, and infrastructure.
- The proposed infrastructure uses federated data with centralized metadata, domain-aware search, common schemas, local "oases", persistent identifiers/DOIs, ELNs, LIMSs, parsers, converters, and ontology-derived workflows.
- The paper explicitly includes heterogeneous catalysis among FAIRmat demonstration areas.

## Relevance
Highly relevant adjacent source for catalysis FAIR infrastructure because it connects NOMAD, FAIRmat, NFDI, metadata schemas, ontologies, ELNs/LIMSs, federated repositories, and AI-ready data. It supports thesis claims that FAIR data require more than repository storage: they require expert-supplied metadata, provenance, domain-aware search, data converters, and community adoption.
