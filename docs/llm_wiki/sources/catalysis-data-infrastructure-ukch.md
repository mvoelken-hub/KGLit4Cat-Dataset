# UK Catalysis Data Infrastructure

**Authors:** Abraham Nieva de la Hidalga, Josephine Goodall, Corinne Anyika, Brian Matthews, C. Richard A. Catlow  
**Year:** 2022  
**Venue:** Catalysis Communications 162:106384  
**File:** `docs/literature/1-s2.0-S1566736721001072-main.pdf`

## Abstract
Short communication describing the UK Catalysis Hub's Catalysis Data Infrastructure (CDI), a prototype infrastructure for cataloguing and linking catalysis publications, datasets, authors, institutions, and research themes. The CDI is designed to align with FAIR data principles by making research outputs more visible, linking heterogeneous data objects to publications, and providing context for reuse.

## Key Findings
- CDI is intentionally not "yet another repository"; it is a catalogue that links data, publications, authors, institutions, and themes.
- Findability and accessibility are supported by cataloguing assets and providing links and identifiers.
- Interoperability and reusability are supported by context, especially links between datasets, publications, software resources, and research workflows.
- Publications do not consistently expose related data objects in metadata, so full-text inspection is often needed to identify data references.
- Many chemistry/catalysis data objects are supplementary and unstructured, which limits interoperability and reuse with minimal human intervention.
- The prototype was used as an engagement tool with researchers and stakeholders to refine requirements and promote data publishing practices.
- Future CDI work includes alignment to PROV-O, DCAT, SPAR, SCHOLIX, FAIRness scoring, and better structure for data objects.

## Relevance
Important catalysis-specific infrastructure source. It supports thesis claims that FAIR catalysis work must connect publications, heterogeneous datasets, software, provenance, and repository records, and that many currently published data objects remain unstructured supplementary material. It is especially relevant to SIMONE's package-level extraction focus because it highlights the gap between publication metadata and actual data-object discovery.
